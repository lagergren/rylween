## Introduction

There is a realm very far away, not necessarily on our world, called
Rylween. Rylween is one of the seven kingdoms of this world. It lies in
the northwestern part of this world's only known continent, surrounded by
impenetrable mountains in every direction. Rumor has it that there is a
canyon somewhere in the southern mountains, but no one has ever found it.
Rylween is an isolated kingdom whose good and just monarch went by the
name Zalkin Ammol.

One day, a lone knight rode into the capital of Rylween, Wilor. He
approached from the south, a purple cloak wrapped around his black.
armor. He demanded to see Ammol, and when they met in Ammol's palace, 
the knight materialized a black trident from the air and pierced Ammol
with it before anyone could react. When the guards tried to attack
the knight, they found him invulnerable to normal weapons. The strange 
knight just stood there, laughing, his near seven foot frame towering 
over anyone at the royal court. He waved his hands and disappeared in a 
puff of smoke. Before he was gone, the royal guard heard him yell:
*"I declare war. This shall be the last part of the realm to fall. 
For I am Baron Ydarg, master of the seven kingdoms, the new ruler 
of Rylween."*

In one night, he built a stronghold somewhere to the southwest: the evil
castle Zadraz. The following weeks were chaotic. Strange and evil creatures
started to appear in the realm, and many good merchants were slain on their
journeys through the forests. All citizens of Rylween were panic-stricken.
The warrior guilds in the four cities of Rylween started to search for
veteran fighters fit to protect the realm. They failed.

This is where you come in.

## About Rylween

Rylween is a single-player role-playing game in the old online tradition.
There is, however, a score list where you can see how well the different
parties succeed. Your final goal is to hunt down Baron Ydarg and destroy
him. He is immune to magic and quite nasty, and this is where the game will
end. Rylween will also create a Hall of Fame with all the winners.

All positions of the different dungeons, people, huts, and so on are
randomized each time a new campaign begins. Thus, it isn't the same game
each time. The missions are the same, but the positions of all the important
locations always vary. Good, eh? I thought quite a lot about how to make an
adventure/RPG interesting after more than one play, and this is what I found
to be the best method.

## Starting the Game

When the game starts you will be at the warrior guild menu. This is where
you create adventurer parties, continue previously saved games, delete
parties you aren't satisfied with, read this document, and so on. If you
are a new player, create a new party here.

You will direct a party of four heroes through Rylween. This is where you
create them. First, enter a name for your party. This name will be used on
the top list, and should be something fantasy-ish.

Now it's time to create the first character. You will see four basic
attributes, and can choose between rerolling them or keeping them. You can
reroll them an infinite number of times, but the initial values can never be
lower than 15 or greater than 70 for any skill.

### Weapon Skill

This is how good the character is in combat. Fighters need a weapon skill
of at least 50.

### Health

This is the character's hit point maximum. Each time a character is wounded,
hit points decrease. If they drop below 0 the character will die. A character
can never get more hit points than health, though.

### Wisdom

This determines how skilled the character is in the arts of magic. The magic
point maximum is equal to the wisdom value. Only priests and mages get magic
points, though. Wisdom is of no importance whatsoever to any of the other
classes. Mages and priests need a wisdom value of at least 50.

### Charisma

This measures the character's general personality and leadership skills.
The party will gain more friends and fewer enemies if it has many characters
with high charisma. Bards need a charisma value of at least 50.

When you have decided you want to keep a good roll, enter a name for this
character. Once again, names with a fantasy flavor will create a better
atmosphere during the game.

## Races

Select a race for your character.

### Human

Well, I hope I don't need to explain this one (except maybe for Alex May).
Humans are a versatile race who can specialize into any character class,
eventually overcoming their inherent limitations. Most of the inhabitants
of Rylween are human.

### Dwarf

Small, stout, bearded humanoids. They make excellent fighters. Dwarves
are excellent smiths and have a close connection to the earth, and know
her secrets. A dwarf can sometimes find gold, that would have been hidden
to any other race. 

### Elf

Tall, slender humanoids. Excellent rangers or mages. Elves are spiritual
creatures, and you also see elven priests, since the elf kind is the
most ancient of races, and are connected to the history of creation and
the gods that sculpted Rylween from primordial chaos.

### Halfling

Short humanoids with hairy feet. Excellent thieves, and their fighting
skill is not to be underestimated. Halflings have an innate ability to
find what is hidden and avoid being detected.

## Classes

Next, pick a class for the character.

### Fighter

Warriors. High-level fighter veterans are fearsome opponents, especially
when you happen to encounter a creature immune to magic. A level 20
fighter with a good weapon can easily kill a foe with one blow. Fighters
have no magic skill.

### Thief

Rogues. Experts in disarming traps and picking locks. Good to have in
your party when exploring dungeons and caves. Thieves have no magic skill.

### Mage

Wizards. Mages are often physically weak, but they are experts in
spellcasting. Mage spells are mainly battle magic:

```text
Frost Finger - Cold damage to one enemy.
Fire Cloud   - Fire damage to every enemy.
Confusion    - Blurs the party and makes enemies less accurate.
Death Strike - Attempts to destroy one enemy outright.
Hold Enemies - Freezes enemies and may buy you a free round.
Anti Magic   - Stops enemy magic for the next attack round.
```

### Priest

Priests are servants of one of many gods of Rylween. They also know
magic, but not the same kind as mages. Priest spells are used from the
wilderness or dungeon command menu:

```text
Healing     - Restores some hit points to one living character.
Restoration - Fully heals one character.
Rebirth     - Raises a dead character, if the priest has enough power.
Banish Evil - Works in dungeons and caves. The less said, the better.
Revelation  - Searches for something special that may be hidden nearby.
Teleport    - Moves the party a chosen number of miles.
Earthquake  - Opens a cave in the wilderness. Do not cast it indoors.
```

### Bard

Bards are experts in telling stories and singing traveler's tunes. A bard
is also often quite a fearsome fighter. Having a bard in your party will
make you popular in the many taverns of Rylween. This means that you may be
able to acquire information that otherwise would be hidden from you.
Bards have no magic skill, but there are many benefits to a good reputation.

### Ranger

Lonely hunters. Rangers are almost as good warriors as fighters. They can
sometimes trap wild animals for the party to eat. This can be an important
skill when you are stranded in the wilderness, far away from any settlement
or place where you can get food. Rangers have no magic skill.

## Entering Rylween

After party creation, your campaign begins in the wilderness near Thord.
Save once you are happy with your new company. It is a cruel world, and
newly minted heroes are not always as sturdy as they think.

## The Map Screen

The map screen will now show an overview of the surrounding countryside.
You are on the highlighted square in the middle. The terrain above is to the
north, the terrain below is to the south, and so on. The map window shows the
nearby land, not the whole realm. The different terrain types are:

```text
/\    =  Mountains. High peaks (impenetrable)
^^    =  Foothills
~~    =  Water (impenetrable)
gg    =  Forests
..    =  Plains
%%    =  Swamps
::    =  Deserts
TT    =  Towns
HH    =  Huts
CC    =  Castles
        (there is only one castle, and I wouldn't enter it too soon)
```

Pressing '?' will bring up a control summary. Here is a more verbose
explanation:

## Wilderness Commands

### C = Camp

You can be awake for about 12 hours without loss of health, but even the
most experienced adventurer needs to sleep. You'll see how it works. A
rule of thumb is to always post a guard. There is always a risk of being
ambushed in your sleep. If you DESPERATELY need the sleep, don't post a
guard. The nights of Rylween can be very cold. Buy some suitable equipment
in the nearest town as soon as possible so that you won't freeze to death.
(I was inspired by the Swedish weather right now).

### E = Enter

Enter a town, castle or hut. You need to be standing on a square of that
kind for this command to work.

### H = Hunt

If you have a ranger in your party, that ranger might trap a rabbit or
another animal for you to eat. This is good if you are starving and out of
food.

### J = View Journal

View the traveler's journal where important coordinates and such are recorded,
so that you don't need to write them down. Nice one.

### L = Leave Item

Discard an item in the party inventory. You'll never see it again.

### P = Put Down Weapon

Just unready a weapon you are wielding.

### Q = Quaff Potion

There are different kinds of potions. I won't tell you what they do, but
the merchant will sell you an average working healing brew. Beware of
strange-colored liquids, but do experiment. Save your game first. There is
no low-level-format potion. Your sysop will rejoice.

### R = Ready Armor

If you have armor in the party inventory and want one of your characters to
wear it, select the armor, and the character will put it on. If already
wearing armor, the character will take that one off, and it will replace the
new armor's position in the inventory.

### S = Save Game

DO THIS OFTEN!  You have one save position. Save your game after you have
found some new equipment. Save your game before entering towns. Save your
game after leaving towns. Save your game before entering that house with
impaled human skulls outside. Save your game whenever it seems appropriate.
You can always go back to your last saved game if you are killed.

### T = Take Off Armor

Take off armor you are wearing.

### V = View Party

A status sheet for one party member or all of them if you want. You will also
see some general information about the party.

### W = Wield Weapon

See ready armor. It's the same idea, but the active objects are weapons.

### X = Quit Game (No Save)

Save before doing this. This will take you back to the title screen. Good if
you were just robbed of all your belongings. Just exit and restore the last
save.

### Z = Cast Spell

Priest magic. See the priest spell list above. You still have to learn when
to use each prayer, and when not to waste the spell points.

### ! - Redraw the Screen

This redraws the screen, should you encounter any glitches in updating
graphics.

### Movement

```text
8 = Move North (or arrow up)

4 = Move West (or arrow left)

6 = Move East (or arrow right)

2 = Move South (or arrow down)
```

## The Four Towns of Rylween

You will start the game near the northwestern part of Rylween. The first
thing you should do is visit the mountain village Thord and buy some
equipment. You are completely defenseless when the game starts.

There are four towns in Rylween:

### Thord - City of the Mountains

In the mountain range to the northwest. I shall be generous and reveal its
coordinates, 12x11.

### Ollk - City of the Swamp

Ollk lies in the Great Swamp in the northeastern part of the realm.

### Wilor - City of the Forest

Wilor is the capital of Rylween. Wilor lies in the center of the realm, in
the large forest known as Lonewood.

### Murn - City of the Desert

Murn lies somewhere in the southeastern desert. Not many travelers have
made it to this part of the land. It is about as far away as you can get
from Thord, and surrounding terrain is hostile.

Travelers you meet in inns and taverns, and on the road, may be able to tell
you more exactly where the towns are located, and how best to prepare for
your travels.

## The Status Bar

That is the thingy at the bottom of the screen.

```text
TIME: What time of the day it is.

ST:   Party Status. OK, Hungry or Starving. When you are starving,
      it is wise to let your ranger (if you have one) trap something
      to eat, or rush to a town to get some food rations at the merchant's.

X:    X coordinate.

Y:    Y coordinate.

FD:   How many food rations you've got left.

MV:   Moves left today.

AU:   (Aurum) - How much gold you have.
```

Below these values are the hit point statistics for the four characters of
the party. H1 is the first character, H2 the second, and so on. 0 = dead.

## Towns

You can visit all the institutions in a town. Here is a small explanation.
Some institutions are not open 24 hours. Please respect this, and keep an eye
on the status bar. Experienced parties may also be asked to pay a small town
tax. Reputation has its price.

### SMITH

The smith is a nice chap. You can buy or sell armor and weapons here. You can
also haggle when selling. Sometimes he might even invite you for a beer. It is
always wise to accept.

### TAVERN

The tavern is always wise to visit. You can buy refreshments, listen to
rumors, raise your reputation by telling stories, and most important, talk to
people. Chitchat sometimes sounds useless. It is not always useless. People
may grant you missions or give you important information. Talk to everyone
you meet! This is the way to success.

### INN

Good to stay at if you are tired. You will restore more hit points than if
you sleep in the wilderness, you won't be murdered in the middle of the
night, and you can have a nice warm supper here. The inn also houses many
important characters you should talk to, and now and then there may be
something interesting for sale.

### MERCHANT

Convenience store. Here, you can buy things like blankets, food rations,
torches, and so on. When buying supplies, you may be asked how many you want.
You can also sell odd items you find, but the merchant won't let you haggle.
The smith is a much nicer guy.

### CHURCH

There are only two churches: in Thord and in Wilor. You can heal wounded
characters here, or raise dead ones. For a price.

## Battles

Battles play an important role in this realm. You may be ambushed when trying
to rest in the wilderness. You may be attacked without warning when exploring
caves. Beasts and bandits are all around, and they are after YOU!

When you encounter one or more creatures, you can choose between fighting and
running. You do not always get away. If steel must be drawn, combat proceeds
in rounds. Each living party member chooses an action, in party order. Dead
characters are skipped. When the party has acted, surviving monsters strike
back, unless you have done something clever enough to stop them for a while.
Some spells and items change the shape of a round rather than simply causing
damage. Learn those timings. They may save your life.

Each character can do a certain number of things. Here is an explanation:

### Attack

Strike at the enemy with your readied weapon.

### Defend

Rest a round and recover a bit, while the other guys do the fighting. This
can also make the enemy's next attack less effective.

### Use Item

Use one of your magical items. The feared Kael's Axe is the most powerful
magic weapon in the realm. Try finding at least ONE Kael's Axe before entering
castle Zadraz.

### Battle Cry

Try to scare your enemies. It works best on cowardly creatures like kobolds,
goblins, etc.

### Cast Spell

Only for mages. The mage can cast one of six battle spells:

```text
Frost Finger - Strike one enemy with pure cold.
Fire Cloud   - Engulf all enemies in burning air.
Confusion    - Makes the party waver and blur in front of the enemy,
               making you more difficult to hit.
Death Strike - KILL! One enemy. Usually.
Hold Enemies - Hold the enemy party so that you get an extra battle
               round.
Anti Magic   - Stop your foes from using magic during the next attack
               round. Good against wizards and magical horrors.
```

The battle is over when all the enemies are dead, or you are dead, or
when you have fled.

Experience points and gold are given to you if you win the battle.

## Dungeons and Caves

There are lots of dungeons and caves scattered throughout the realm.
You may stumble upon them by chance, or you may know where to go.
Dungeons play an important role in the different missions you can receive.
A dungeon/cave consists of an unknown number of levels. To enter the
next level, choose (D)escend from the dungeon menu. To return to
the previous level, choose (A)scend. Dungeons are full of all kinds
of nice monsters that don't exist up in the wilderness.
You can use all the standard wilderness commands in the dungeon.

Bring torches, food, and patience. Searching below ground costs time, and
some levels hold nothing except trouble. Others hold things that no honest
merchant is likely to keep under the counter. A thief helps with traps and
locks; a priest helps when the walls start whispering.

## Missions

The missions are the heart of this game. It is quite meaningless just to roam
the land for fame and treasure (although that also is a possibility). Try to
solve the quests you are granted. Therefore, it is always wise to talk to all
the people you meet at taverns, inns, or in the wilderness. Some have
important clues, some grant you missions, some are not important at all. Each
mission is a "trail" you must follow.

> HINT: If you want some quick experience and money, try the first mission.
> Go to THORD, City of the mountains and talk to Garth, who resides
> in this city in ALL the campaigns. He will grant you a simple
> starting quest.

## Attitudes

Just like in real life, you can pick your path. Here are a few suggestions:

### Bounty Hunters

Your party roams the wilderness, searching for fame and fortune.
The only goal is to get as mighty as possible.

### Freedom Force

Your party does its very utmost to assassinate Baron Ydarg, the evil
ruler of Rylween. This is the main purpose of the game, but you don't
NEED to kill him if you don't want to.

When Ydarg dies, the campaign will restart, and the victorious party
will enter the Hall of Fame.

### Neutral

Just be neutral. Don't cooperate with anyone special. Be yourselves.

## The Power Items

To kill the evil Baron Ydarg, you will need more than courage. The five power
items are: The Master Crown, the Fire Shield, the Foot of Jazzy, the Silver
Key, and the Bracelets of Thun. When you have acquired all of these through
different missions, you are ready to find Ydarg's castle Zadraz and breach
its defenses...
